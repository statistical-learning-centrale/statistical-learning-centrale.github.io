Course Description

Sparsity is a ubiquitous concept in statistics, signal processing and machine learning. The course studies sparse recovery by convex relaxation. We start on a grid, with L1-minimisation, the null space property and dual certificates. We then move off the grid, to sparse measures and the Beurling LASSO (BLASSO). The focus is on estimation guarantees: exact recovery without noise, prediction and localisation bounds with noise, and stability of the support at low noise. The last lecture treats the generalised BLASSO with Fenchel-Young losses. Two examples run through the course: super-resolution from Fourier measurements, and the estimation of the means of a Gaussian mixture.

Course material

  • Lecture notes. 2027 lecture notes (PDF, version of 16 November 2026). They cover the five lectures. A sixth chapter on algorithms supports the projects. The 2026 notes (first two lectures of the former syllabus) remain available.
  • Complement: a first look at the BLASSO. An introduction to sparse spikes recovery via the BLASSO (PDF), notes by Clarice Poon, written for a course at the Institut Henri Poincaré (IHP). They give a first idea of what the BLASSO is. The course builds on these notes and goes further.
  • Prerequisite: optimisation. Optimisation notes (PDF). These are the notes of the optimisation course that the students of the MIR option at Centrale Lyon follow in January 2027. Other students should have taken a course in optimisation before. This prerequisite is light. If optimisation is new to you, this is not a problem: the notes will help you go deeper.
  • Projects. Guidelines and suggested references.
  • Past exams. 2023 exam, 2025 exam, 2026 exam. The topic of the course has shifted slightly, so the exams change from 2027 on: they will no longer bear on the same technical points. These past exams are given for information only. Do not rely on them too much.
